# Illustrative session: diagnosis without repair

> Synthetic composite. Names and sources are invented to demonstrate the protocol.

## Contract

- Seed: a neighborhood repair-café event page
- Budget: 6 meaningful hops
- Mode: guided
- Checkpoint: every 3 hops
- Non-goal: do not produce an article topic

## Trail

### 1. Event page → volunteer repair log

- Observation: the event page links to a public log of attempted repairs.
- Unfamiliar detail: the log distinguishes “fixed,” “not fixed,” and “diagnosed.”
- Next-hop reason: “diagnosed” is treated as an outcome rather than a failed repair.

### 2. Repair log → intake form

- Observation: the intake form asks whether the owner wants “cause identification only.”
- Inference: some visitors may value understanding the failure even when repair is impossible.
- Next-hop reason: follow the service category rather than another repair technique.

### 3. Intake form → volunteer training note

- Observation: volunteers are instructed to explain failure causes and future options before closing a case.
- Human intervention: “Do not turn this into a right-to-repair argument. Follow why explanation itself counts.”

## Checkpoint

- Route: public event → repair outcomes → intake category → volunteer practice.
- Repetition warning: all sources come from one organization.
- Open route chosen by human: find first-hand accounts from owners whose devices were not repaired.

### 4. Training note → owner forum post

- Observation: an owner reports relief after learning that data was recoverable even though the device was not.
- Uncertainty: a single personal account cannot establish prevalence.
- Next-hop reason: the value may be uncertainty reduction rather than physical repair.

### 5. Owner post → data-recovery checklist

- Observation: the checklist separates device function, data access and replacement decisions.
- Explanation revision: “repair success” is not one binary outcome.
- Next-hop reason: examine whether repair communities record these non-binary outcomes.

### 6. Checklist → community outcome taxonomy

- Observation: the taxonomy includes diagnosis, safe disposal, data recovery, skill transfer and successful repair.
- New question: why do repair services and statistics often collapse these different forms of value into “fixed / not fixed”?

## Retrospective

The initial object was a repair event. The unforeseen route was not a new repair technique but an alternative outcome model: diagnosis can reduce uncertainty, protect data or improve replacement decisions even when the object remains broken. The human intervention prevented a familiar policy frame from absorbing the trail. The next session should leave this synthetic environment and test whether real organizations record similar outcomes.
